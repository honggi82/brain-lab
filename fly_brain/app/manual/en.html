Hosted edition manual

Explore the fly connectome

An educational viewer of precomputed experiments, not a validated prediction of biological movement.

Quick start

  1. Select one of six presets or a population in the circuit.
  2. Change path hops from 1 to 10 to replay that result. Re-run preserves the current experiment and hop count.
  3. Clear removes the experiment and selection; changing hops afterwards does not restore it.
  4. Click a soma to inspect its identity, then replay its population.

Hosted versus local

Hosted mode supports presets, populations and path hops. Neuron search, individual-neuron simulation and arbitrary parameters require the local Python backend. Duration 1s, tau 50ms, gain 2 and stimulus length 250ms are fixed. The last stored rate frame is at 0.995s.

The descending sample contains the 200 neurons with the greatest input plus output synapse count. Select the descending circuit population for all 1,316. All circuit population recordings use path/downstream mode.

Body and colour

On mobile, Load full body model downloads about 13MB on demand. Desktop loads it when the body panel becomes visible.

Joint posture follows the current frame. Body colour and the table show final-time excitation plus signed (negative) inhibition. Intensity and ordering use the absolute net value.

Midline neck/abdomen pitch uses the mean of left and right posture values. Lateral axes named abduct or twist use half of left minus right. Original axes, limits and joint weights remain unchanged. Midline colour uses mean bilateral motor output. This is an explicit visualisation assumption, not measured motor control.

The 3D view contains 141,781 positioned somata; calculations include 211,577 annotated neurons. Stimulus markers are capped at 4,000 and frame activity at 2,500 for display, independently of the full population count.

01What it does

It puts 211,577 central-nervous-system neurons and the 26,028,386 synaptic connections between them into one weighted graph, and propagates signal across it.

QuestionWhat you do
If I stimulate this neuron, how far does the signal reach?Click a soma in the 3D view

02What it is built from

Three public sources. All free, none needs a login.

2.1 The connectome — MaleCNS v1.0

An electron-microscopy connectome of the entire male fly central nervous system: brain, optic lobes and ventral nerve cord. Released in September 2026 by FlyEM / HHMI Janelia, the University of Cambridge, the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology and Google Research. About 166,700 neurons and 125 million synapses, licensed CC BY 4.0.

FileSizeUsed for
connectome-weights-…feather1.1 GBConnections and synapse counts (the weights)
body-neurotransmitters-…feather42 MBTransmitter predictions → excitatory/inhibitory sign
body-annotations-…feather13 MBCell type, soma position, body part, side
One trap

The connection file holds 151.86 M edges, but 125.83 M of them run to unannotated segmentation fragments rather than traced neurons. Keep them and the node count balloons to 88.4 M and the result stops meaning anything. So only edges whose both ends are annotated neurons are kept.

2.2 The body — Flybody

A whole-body MuJoCo model of the fly from Google DeepMind and HHMI Janelia, built from confocal microscopy at 0.3 to 2 µm, with 67 segments and 102 degrees of freedom. github.com/TuragaLab/flybody (Apache-2.0)

It is the most detailed fly body released publicly. NeuroMechFly v2 (FlyGym) is built from micro-CT and ships its segments capped at 2,000 faces each, 3.8 MB of STL in total. The FlyGym package itself carries Flybody's fruitfly.xml alongside its own model.

The ceiling on public resolution

The 22.6 M-face hi-poly version in the paper was a modelling intermediate and was never released as a mesh. The Figshare dataset (10.25378/janelia.25309105) holds the raw confocal stack and training data instead. So GitHub's 272,550 triangles is the highest published resolution, and this program ships all of it, unsimplified.

2.3 The shell — fly-connectome-template

The starting point for the 3D viewer structure and the attribution notice. github.com/cobanov/fly-connectome-template

Why the sources were fetched separately

The template has no connection data. It carries 140,024 soma positions and nothing else, and its bundled Flybody mesh is a reduced one: 93,879 triangles, two front legs, no joints.

3.1 Building the graph — about 8 minutes

  1. Take the 211,577 annotated neurons as nodes.
  2. Keep only edges whose both ends are nodes → 26,028,386.
  3. Turn the transmitter prediction into a sign. The sign belongs to the presynaptic cell (Dale's principle).
  4. Store forward, backward and unsigned magnitude as three scipy CSR matrices.
  5. Convert soma positions to display space — one uniform scale, fitted over the whole CNS including the nerve cord, not the brain alone. That is what makes a signal travelling from brain to nerve cord visible on screen.

3.2 Propagation

Two modes over the same graph.

Path mode — discrete hops

The matrix is normalised by output, so each entry reads “what fraction of this neuron's output arrives here”, and hub neurons do not dominate the result by sheer size. Each hop applies a decay, and the amount arriving (magnitude) is tracked separately from its polarity (sign) — otherwise opposing paths cancel a neuron out of the picture entirely.

Rate mode — continuous time

One leaky integrator per neuron.

r ← r + (dt / τ) · ( tanh( max(0, W·r · gain + stimulus) ) − r )

Here the matrix is normalised by input, so each entry reads “what fraction of this neuron's input comes from there”, and the drive a neuron receives stays bounded by 1 even when hundreds of partners fire at once.

Why the two normalisations differ

Rate mode first used output normalisation, and 50,000 neurons saturated flat at 1.0, carrying no information at all. For a rate model, input normalisation is not a preference — it is required.

3.3 Converting the body model

The MJCF (fruitfly.xml) is parsed for the body tree, joint axes, joint limits and mesh assignments; the 85 OBJ meshes are moved into each body's own frame and written as one binary.

  • Physics-only collision primitives (class="collision") are skipped — they would hide the model.
  • MuJoCo quaternions are (w,x,y,z), three.js wants (x,y,z,w).
  • A joint does not rotate about its segment's origin, so each is wrapped in a pivot pair (+joint position → rotate → −joint position) to turn exactly about the axis MuJoCo specifies.
  • The default is no decimation: 272,550 triangles, 13.1 MB.

3.4 Motor neurons to joints — the core

MaleCNS names each motor neuron after the muscle it innervates, and those muscles come in antagonist pairs across one joint. So the name alone assigns the joint. 669 of the 815 motor neurons land this way.

Beyond the legs: 67 wing, 214 abdominal, 44 neck and 16 haltere motor neurons group per body part.

balance    = (agonist − antagonist) / (agonist + antagonist)   → −1 … +1
engagement = √( this joint's drive / the run's strongest joint drive )
angle       = balance × engagement × joint range
Why engagement is in there

A joint whose antagonist pool is unnamed in the annotations (thorax–coxa adduction, for one) has a balance permanently at ±1. Without the engagement term, a single faintly active neuron slams that joint to its limit and the fly looks like it is convulsing. The first implementation did exactly that.

An angle of 0 is the model's own rest pose, so an undriven joint simply stays put.

6.1 Reading the circuit panel

03 / Circuit, below the body view, is the same connectome seen as populations rather than neurons. 211,577 neurons cannot be drawn, so they are grouped into 44 functional populations and every synapse between two groups is summed into one edge.

ColumnWhat is in it
sensory inputvisual · olfactory · gustatory · head mechanosensory, plus neck · wing · haltere · abdomen and six legs (left/right × front, middle, hind)

Edge width is the real sum of synapses and the colour is how much of that sum is excitatory. Click a population to keep only its connections; its ten strongest partners appear on the right with numbers. The min-synapse slider filters the weak links away. When a simulation runs, these populations light up with it — cyan for net excitation, orange for net inhibition — and an edge only brightens when both of its ends are lit.

A population's brightness is the mean activation per neuron in it, not the sum. A sum would rank populations by size, and the optic lobe's 93,396 neurons would top every run whether or not the signal actually went there.

Brain regions come from the MaleCNS class field and from the prefix of the type name, which encodes the neuropil — Tm and Mi are medulla, LC is lobula columnar, and AVLP, SMP and GNG are the ventrolateral protocerebrum, superior protocerebrum and gnathal ganglion.

6.2 Clicking a population to stimulate it

Clicking a population in the circuit stimulates every neuron in it and runs the propagation immediately — the same thing clicking a soma does in 01 / Nervous system, except the whole group is the starting point. The neuron IDs never travel: the server receives only the population name and looks its members up, because one population holds 93,396 neurons and shipping those IDs out and back would cost more than the run itself.

Population clickedNeurons stimulatedStrongest motor output after 3 hops
L front leg sensory562L front leg motor — the leg that was touched
descending1,316wing, then neck — flight steering
optic lobe93,396neck motor — turning the head toward what is seen

A large population naturally spreads far. Switching on the entire optic lobe is not something that happens in a fly; it is the thought experiment "if this whole region were active, how far would it reach". Drop the hops to 2 or 3 to see only the local circuit.

6.3 Layers, and the edges that run backwards

The six columns arrange the diagram so the path from sensory to motor reads left to right. They are not the order signal travels. This nervous system is not feedforward: 134,766 of the 211,577 neurons, 64%, are interneurons, and 113 of the 411 aggregated edges run against the column order.

Those edges are drawn rather than hidden — bowed upward as purple dashed curves, and the Back edges button in the control row toggles them. The heaviest is ascending to descending, 570,000 synapses, the path by which information arriving from the body reaches the very next motor command. That recurrence is why the diagram is not a picture to read once, left to right.

Wiring versus arrangement

Edge width and colour are measurements. The thickest edge is optic lobe to lobula output at 5.69 M synapses, and olfactory receptor neurons to antennal lobe comes out 100% excitatory, matching the fact that they are all cholinergic. The six columns and their order are a reading arrangement, not a measured hierarchy.

The price of grouping

44 populations flatten 211,577 neurons. One block labelled "ventrolateral protocerebrum" holds 5,520 neurons and hundreds of cell types, and none of that structure shows in the circuit. For the single-neuron level, click somata in 01 / Nervous system instead.

Clicking a point in panel 01 stimulates that neuron and runs immediately. Shift-click adds more (that does not auto-run, so press “Re-run” when the set is ready).

7.3 Picking by search

The 04 / Detail → search tab finds neurons by type name, instance or body ID. Filter by role, body part and side, then “Stimulate all N shown” for a population stimulus — role motor + part front leg + left gives the whole left front leg motor pool.

Two settings that decide the answer

Hops is the big one. One hop from a leg's sensory neurons reaches 1,440 neurons and moves that leg. Five hops reaches 36,321 — 17% of the nervous system — and by then the signal has gone up to the brain and back down, so every limb moves. Pick the hop count that matches the question you are asking.

Keep gain near 2.0. It scales input-normalised drive, so anything above about 6 saturates every neuron.

7.5 How long it takes

  • Path mode: about 1 second
  • Rate mode: about 4 seconds at the default 200 steps
  • The first run after changing direction, min synapses or inhibition pays an extra normalisation pass.

08Reading the result

8.1 The flow tab

The optic lobe / central brain / descending / nerve cord share bars tell you whether the signal actually left the brain without reading any coordinates. The descending, ascending, motor and sensory counts below them are how much passed each gateway.

8.2 The body panel

  • Drive / brake — the excitatory and inhibitory sums over that limb's motor neurons
  • Joint balance — + is extension, promotion, levation; − is the opposite
  • Sensory reached — which body parts' sensory neurons carried this signal

Past five hops it fades rather than spreads

The count reached peaks at 36,321 neurons on hop 5 and falls to 27,339 by hop 10. The 0.85 per-hop decay compounds (0.20x by hop 10) until weak arrivals drop below the threshold. Hops beyond five do not carry the signal further; they redistribute strength among places it already reached.

The fly's nervous system is only a few synapses wide. Past two hops a leg stimulus is no longer a leg reflex — it is the animal's whole postural response, plus the model's own fan-out. See §10 for what part of that is biology and what is artefact.

09Measured vs modelled

Keeping this distinction is the most important thing about this program.

measured taken as published

  • 211,577 neurons and 26,028,386 connections, weighted by real synapse counts
  • Soma positions, cell types, superclasses, body parts, sides
  • Transmitter predictions (Janelia's own)
  • 67 body segments, joint axes and limits, 272,550 mesh triangles

modelled assumptions laid on top

  • Sign assignment — the transmitter-to-polarity rule
  • Propagation — fraction-of-output diffusion or leaky rate units
  • Joint angle — balance × engagement
  • Body mapping — motor neuron name to joint

This is not recorded physiology. Spike timing, neuropeptides, gap junctions, synaptic plasticity, muscle force, gravity and ground contact are all absent. It is kinematics, not physics. The fly does not walk.

10Limits

  1. 47,206 neurons with an unknown transmitter are assumed weakly excitatory at +0.5. That is 22% of them.
  2. 146 of the 815 motor neurons cannot be pinned to a joint by name and group only by body part.
  3. Multi-hop spread overstates how far a real signal travels. Hop diffusion has no refractory period, no threshold and no time, so influence accumulates without the brakes a real circuit has. At high hop counts hub neurons win — the wing power muscle motor neurons receive from so many premotor cells that after five hops they outrank the leg that was actually touched. Read runs past three hops as reachability, not as a prediction of movement.
  4. Neurons without a soma position are not drawn (141,781 of 211,577 have one). They take part in the propagation normally.
  5. Gap junctions are not in the connectome. The giant fibre circuit uses them, so the real speed of the escape response is not reproduced.
  6. A male connectome is paired with a female body model (MaleCNS is male; Flybody was built from a single female). Take care with sexually dimorphic circuits.
